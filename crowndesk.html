The idea

Three products, built around real student problems.

Students often juggle job applications, demanding coursework, and the challenge of finding their people in a new city. I gave each problem its own product, then connected the thinking behind them. I also worked through the less visible but important parts: secure accounts, admin checks, reliable data, and AI features that have a clear purpose.

At the centre A shared hub for routing, status checks, and admin tools

01 / Challenge

Too many disconnected student tools

Career planning, difficult study material, and community support usually live in separate places. CrownDesk explores how those parts of student life can feel more connected.

01 - Keeping job searches organised

JobDesk

JobDesk began with a problem I had myself: my applications were spread across emails, notes, and folders. I wanted one place where I could see what I had applied for, keep the right documents close, compare my CV with a role, and remember when to follow up.

See every application and where it stands Bring application data in or take it out with CSV Keep CVs and cover letters together Compare a CV with the job description Prepare emails, interviews, and follow-ups with AI support Use reminders, company research, and progress analytics
FlaskSQLAlchemyChart.jsBootstrapCSV
Engineering focus

A Flask and SQLAlchemy application that keeps applications, documents, reminders, and analytics connected around one career workflow.

What I learned

A useful dashboard starts with a clear data model. Statuses, dates, documents, and follow-ups need to work together before the charts can add value.

02 - Making medical study more interactive

MediNest

With MediNest, I wanted to explore how technology could make dense medical subjects easier to work through. Its fifteen modules let students ask questions, practise clinical cases, think through diagnoses, translate material, and look up trusted medical references without jumping between a dozen tools.

Ask questions with an AI study assistant Work through clinical cases and diagnosis exercises Explore anatomy, drug information, and safety checks Translate study material with DeepL Look up ICD-11 information and PubMed research Track health, medication, rotations, wellness, and visa tasks
Next.jsReactFastAPIPostgreSQLClaude
Engineering focus

A Next.js interface backed by modular FastAPI routers, PostgreSQL, and Redis, with separate services for study tools, trackers, authentication, and external APIs.

What I learned

Medical software needs clear boundaries. Educational AI, source lookup, disclaimers, and modular services all matter when a feature touches health information.

03 - Helping students find their people

Nexora

Moving somewhere new can feel isolating, especially when you do not yet know anyone at your university. I designed Nexora to help students discover people with a shared university, country, or interest, then get to know them through posts, conversations, and short-lived stories.

Find students through shared interests and backgrounds Connect through posts, comments, chat, and stories Join communities, discover events, and explore nearby activity Confirm new accounts with an email code Keep the community student-focused through ID review Support moderation with admin tools, limits, and audit views
FlaskSQLAuthGroqClaude
Engineering focus

A Flask-based social platform with profiles, feeds, communities, events, direct messaging, OTP sign-up, student-ID review, and a separate admin surface.

What I learned

Community products are not only about engagement. Identity checks, moderation, rate limits, and transparent admin actions are part of the product experience.

Engineering the ecosystem

The difficult part was not building three pages. It was making three products behave like one system.

CrownDesk gave me experience with the parts of software that sit between a feature and a dependable product: authentication, routing, data ownership, usage limits, admin visibility, failure states, and deployment configuration.

Architecture

Modular services

The hub fronts independently deployable Flask and FastAPI applications, so one product can evolve without forcing every service to change at once.

Security

Accounts need layers of protection

Hashed passwords, OTP email verification, CSRF protection, rate limiting, production secret checks, and admin review form the security baseline.

Data

Different products, different state

SQLAlchemy models support the Flask products, PostgreSQL backs larger structured workloads, and Redis helps where fast temporary state is useful.

AI and APIs

Useful only with a clear job

Claude, Groq, DeepL, ICD-11, and PubMed integrations are attached to specific workflows, with limits and safety messaging where the context demands it.

Operations

Admin and service visibility

Separate admin views support user review, analytics, audit history, feedback, broadcasts, and service-health awareness across the ecosystem.

Delivery

Designed beyond local development

Gunicorn, Docker, nginx, environment-based configuration, and Render deployment shaped how routes, secrets, and service boundaries were designed.

What CrownDesk changed for me

Three lessons I will carry into a development team

Start with the workflow.

The technology becomes easier to choose when the user's real problem is clear.

Security is product work.

Verification, limits, review tools, and safe defaults shape whether people can trust an application.

Own the whole path.

Following a feature from interface to database to deployment exposes the trade-offs that isolated coding exercises miss.
